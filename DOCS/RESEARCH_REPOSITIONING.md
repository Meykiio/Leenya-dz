# Leenya Repositioning Research

Compiled from web research, competitor analysis, and pricing data across Algeria and MENA.
Date: July 2026

---

## Q1: Who is the real buyer, and what do they actually pay for tools today?

### What dentists/clinics/salons currently use in Algeria

**WhatsApp Business is the default.** It's not a choice — it's how everyone communicates with patients. For clinics especially, WhatsApp handles appointment booking, reminders, two-way confirmation, post-treatment follow-up, and recall. Multiple vendors (DentinCloud, MedisyncFlow, NiftyHMS, Whautomate, ManyContacts) now build specifically on top of WhatsApp Business API to serve dental clinics globally, including MENA. The evidence strongly suggests that **WhatsApp, not another SaaS app, is the real competitor** Leenya would displace for clinic appointments.

**For dental-specific software:** One Algeria-specific player exists — **dentdz**. It's a cloud-based all-in-one dental practice management system (patient records, appointments, billing, inventory, labs, analytics). Available in French, Arabic, English. They offer a free trial but pricing is not public. They're newer and directly targeting the Algerian dental market. This is the closest direct competitor for the clinic/dentist segment.

**For beauty booking:** Three Algeria-specific apps exist:
- **Beautify** (beautify.dz) — "Algeria's first beauty booking app." Consumer app for clients + Beautify Business dashboard for salon owners. Features: 24/7 booking, real-time availability, automatic reminders, revenue stats, full calendar. They offer 1 month free on signup, then paid. Mobile app has 44 MB, 3.7 stars, from developer Fethi Mezdour. Base in Oran.
- **Dalti** (dalti.dz) — "First Algerian app for booking salons, barbers, stylists." Covers 58 wilayas.
- **Djamaly** (djamaly.com) — Similar booking platform for hair, nails, lashes, beauty.

**Fresha has a presence in Algeria** — it lists beauty salons, hair salons, and medspas in Algeria with online booking. A medical aesthetics clinic in Algiers lists PRP at 12,000 DZD and filler at 130,000 DZD on Fresha.

**For general medical appointments:**
- **Doctome** (doctome.dz) — online medical appointment booking platform in Algeria.
- **Yadoct** (yadoct.com) — "Coming Soon" healthcare platform for Algeria with appointment booking + live queue tracking. Targeting Algiers launch.

### Real per-visit revenue for target segments in Algeria (DZD)

**Dentists/clinics (from DentoLink, March 2026 detailed analysis):**
| Procedure | Average fee (DZD) |
|---|---|
| Simple extraction | 1,500 – 3,000 |
| Composite filling | 4,000 – 8,000 |
| Scaling/cleaning | 3,000 – 6,000 |
| Zirconia crown | 30,000 – 60,000 |
| Laser whitening | 15,000 – 30,000 |

A dentist's per-visit revenue: **1,500 – 8,000 DZD** for routine care, up to **60,000+ DZD** for major procedures. This is 2–13× the barber's 300–1,000 DZD per haircut.

**Higher-end beauty salons (from Fresha Algeria listings):**
- PRP treatment: 12,000 DZD
- Hair treatment: 2,000 – 12,000 DZD

- Filler: 130,000 DZD
- Standard haircut (women's): ~1,500 – 4,000 DZD
- Chignons (bridal): 7,000 DZD

Most higher-end salon services run **2,000 – 15,000 DZD** per visit.

### Are these businesses appointment-first?

**Dentists and clinics are almost entirely appointment-first** — walk-ins are the exception, not the rule. Dental appointments are typically scheduled days or weeks ahead. The queue concept is largely irrelevant to them. Key pain points: no-shows (clinics report 35–50% no-show reduction from WhatsApp reminders), double-booking, phone-tag scheduling, and the administrative burden of managing appointments via WhatsApp DMs.

**Higher-end beauty salons** are mixed: some walk-in for quick services (nails, brows) but appointment-first for major services (bridal, color, treatments). The queue feature has moderate relevance for nail/walk-in services but low relevance for the high-ticket services.

**Clinics have virtually no walk-in culture.** The queue is nearly irrelevant. Confirms the "bookings over queue" thesis strongly for clinics, and moderately for higher-end salons.

### Social media & bio link presence

- **Instagram:** 14.15 million users in Algeria (Dec 2025), growing 5.1% in 3 months. 52% men, 48% women. Largest age group: 25–34 (5.2M users).
- **TikTok:** 24.8 million adult users (80.1% of adults 18+). Growing 22.6% YoY. Younger skew: 56% are 18–24.
- **Facebook:** Still anchor platform at 84.9% market share by traffic, but Instagram/TikTok dominate engagement.
- **WhatsApp:** Ubiquitous. The default business communication channel.

Social commerce is "the fastest-growing economic channel in Algeria." Most micro-merchants operate via Instagram/TikTok discovery → WhatsApp for ordering. Businesses with a digital presence are common in Algiers, Oran, Constantine. Outside major cities, Instagram/TikTok business presence is lower but growing rapidly (TikTok grew 22.6% in a year).

**Verdict:** The Instagram/TikTok bio-link distribution bet is well-supported by adoption data. A bio link is already a familiar concept for any business that posts on social media. Outside major cities, adoption is lower but the growth trajectory is strong.

## Q2: Competitive landscape — Algeria and MENA specifically

### Direct competitors in Algeria

| Competitor | Segment | Model | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **dentdz** | Dental clinics | SaaS, cloud, free trial | Closest clinic competitor. All-in-one: appointments, billing, inventory, labs, analytics, patient records. Fr/Ar/En. |
| **Beautify** | Beauty salons | Consumer app + pro dashboard | 1 month free then paid. 3.7 stars. Base in Oran. Consumer marketplace model. |
| **Dalti** | Salons/barbers | Consumer app + booking | Covers 58 wilayas. |
| **Djamaly** | Beauty | Booking platform | Hair, nails, lashes, beauty. |
| **Doctome** | Medical | Online appointment booking | General medical appointments. |
| **Yadoct** | Medical | Booking + live queue tracking | Coming soon, Algiers. |

### The real competitor: WhatsApp Business

WhatsApp Business is the ubiquitous, zero-cost alternative for every segment. For clinics specifically:
- **50–70% open rates** on reminders (vs 22% for SMS)
- **60% no-show reduction** with automated reminders
- **24/7 booking** via chatbot
- **Cost:** WhatsApp Business API ~$0.03–$0.08 per template message

**What WhatsApp does NOT do** that Leenya could add:
- No structured multi-service booking page (just chat threads)
- No automated queue management/position tracking
- No public business profile page (services, pricing, availability)
- No staff assignment logic (who's available when)
- No analytics (revenue per service, no-show rate, busiest hours)
- No patient history or visit records
- No waitlist management
- No cancellation/rescheduling workflows
- Each WhatsApp conversation is a one-off — no persistent booking state

The opportunity is clear: **Leenya would be what comes after WhatsApp** — the structured layer on top of the messaging habit, not a replacement for it.

### Global competitors present in MENA

- **Fresha:** Has actual listings in Algeria (salons, medspas). Free core + $14.95–$19.95/mo paid. 20% commission on marketplace-acquired new clients. Best for beauty, not clinics.
- **Booksy:** $29.99/mo + $20/staff. No Algeria-specific presence found.
- **Setmore:** Free for up to 4 users, Pro at $5–$12/user/mo. Generic scheduling, no Algeria-specific presence.
- **Square Appointments:** Free for solo, $29–$69/mo for teams. No Algeria-specific presence.

### Clinic-specific software internationally

Global dental software (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Curve Dental) starts at $40–$399/mo. None have Algeria-specific pricing or presence. dentdz is the only Algeria-targeted dental software found.

## Q3: Pricing validation

### "Trivial" price math for each segment

**Clinics/dentists:** Per-visit revenue 1,500–8,000 DZD. A subscription of **1,000–2,000 DZD/mo** = 0.1–1.3 visits/month. For a dentist seeing 5–15 patients/day, this is genuinely trivial — less than half a day's revenue from one patient.

**Higher-end salons:** Per-visit 2,000–15,000 DZD. Same math works at 1,000–2,000 DZD/mo.

**Barbershops (current customer):** 300–1,000 DZD/visit. Even 1,000 DZD/mo = 1–3 haircuts. Still not "trivial" but less offensive than 4,900 DZD.

### Recommended pricing structure

**Two tiers:**
- **Free** — roughly current scope: public business page, booking widget, queue (simplified), basic reminders, 1 staff member, X appointments/month
- **Paid** — 1,990 DZD/mo (~$15/mo) — unlimited appointments, multiple staff, advanced reminders, analytics, no branding

Rationale: 1,990 DZD/mo is ~$15 which matches Fresha/Booksy/Setmore pricing globally. In DZD it's "two good haircuts" or "one scaling session." For a 10-patient/day dentist: 0.3% of monthly revenue.

**Cost structure sanity check (paid tier at 1,990 DZD/mo):**
- Supabase: $25/mo free tier; $25–$99/mo Pro for moderate volume. At 1,990 DZD (~$15), even 100 paid users = ~$1,500/mo → covers Supabase Pro easily.
- Vercel: $20/mo Pro. Same math works.
- Sentry: Free for low volume, $26/mo for teams.
- **Heavy user cost:** A 10-staff clinic with 1,000 bookings/mo would add DB reads/writes. Supabase Pro ($25/mo) handles 8GB bandwidth, 250M requests. Blow past that and you're at $200/mo for the Scale tier. With 100 paid customers, that's $1,990/mo revenue — still profitable.

**Risk:** A genuinely heavy multi-location clinic could cost $50–$100/mo to serve. If the flat price is $15/mo (1,990 DZD), that's a loss. **Mitigation:** Keep the invisible safety ceiling — if a user exceeds X bookings/month, they hit a rate limit or get prompted to contact sales. Don't advertise it, but don't let it go uncapped either.

### Free tier lessons from competitors

| Platform | Free tier limits |
|---|---|
| **Setmore** | Up to 4 users, 200 appointments/mo, branded booking page, email reminders |
| **Square Appts** | Solo only, booking page, reminders, no team features |
| **Fresha** | Core booking + scheduling (but 20% marketplace commission + payment processing fees) |
| **Booksy** | No free tier. $29.99/mo min. |
| **SimplyBook.me** | Free for 1 staff, 50 bookings/mo (limited) |

**Recommendation for Leenya free tier:**
- 1 staff member (the owner)
- 100 appointments/mo (covers small clinics/salons starting out)
- Booking page + basic reminders
- Queue (if relevant)
- Staff-branded version (for paid, remove branding)
- This is a credible acquisition tool without cannibalizing paid

## Q4: Bookings-vs-queue product priority

### What causes the most pain for appointment-driven businesses

The global evidence (from ~60 clinics on Go4whatsup, Adit case studies, dental industry surveys) ranks pain points:

1. **No-shows** — cited by 85%+ of clinics as the #1 operational cost. Automated reminders reduce by 35–60%.
2. **Phone-tag scheduling** — receptionists spending 40–60% of their day on phone bookings. Online self-booking eliminates this.
3. **Double-booking** — especially with multi-staff WhatsApp chaos.
4. **Walk-in/schedule conflicts** — minor for clinics, moderate for salons.

**The queue has essentially zero relevance for clinics.** For higher-end salons, it's a minor feature (for nail/walk-in services). For barbershops, it remains relevant.

### What competitors lead with in their marketing

- **dentdz** (dental, Algeria): "Transform your practice with intelligent management" — leads with appointments, patient records, billing, analytics. Queue: not mentioned.
- **Beautify** (beauty, Algeria): "Your beauty, perfectly booked" — leads with 24/7 booking, reminders, multi-service. Queue: not mentioned.
- **Fresha**: "Instantly book salons and spas" — marketplace discovery + booking.
- **Setmore**: "Free scheduling software" — online booking, reminders, payments.
- **Booksy**: "Scheduling software" — online booking, client management, marketing.

**None of the competitors leading the appointment-driven segment mention a queue.** The queue is purely a barbershop/walk-in feature.

**Recommendation:** Leenya's landing page and product should lead with **bookings** (scheduling, reminders, no-show reduction). The queue becomes a secondary feature toggle for businesses that need it (barbershops, walk-in salons). Internally, the product is a booking platform that also has a queue module.

## Q5: Bio-link distribution motion

### How service businesses currently use bio links

The pattern is well-established globally and in MENA:
- Instagram bio → Linktree / native multi-link → booking page, WhatsApp, menu/services, Instagram shop
- Booking tools (Setmore, Booksy, Fresha, YouCanBookMe, BookedIN, Appointedd, Reservio) all have "add your booking link to your Instagram bio" as a standard integration
- Linktree now has a native **Bookings** feature that connects to Google Calendar — they're moving into the scheduling space

**Key insight:** The bio link slot is already familiar. The ask is "put this link in your bio" not "change how you run your business." For a clinic or salon that already posts on Instagram, adding a link is zero-friction.

### Examples of booking-first bio-link positioning

- **Setmore**: "Share your online Booking Page and every new appointment lands in your calendar." They market the booking page as a link you put anywhere — website, Instagram, Facebook.
- **Reservio**: "Booking link checklist: Instagram, Google, and email" — entire guide on how to use a booking link as your primary customer acquisition tool.
- **BookedIN**: CRM + scheduling product that positions itself primarily as an Instagram bio link replacement for appointment booking.
- **Linktree Bookings**: Native scheduling inside Linktree — the bio-link company is now also a scheduling company. This confirms the convergence.

### What the public page needs to be

For a 16–25-year-old Algerian Instagram user tapping a bio link:
- **Loads in <2 seconds** — mobile-only assumption. Should be a lightweight SPA or static page.
- **Visual polish** — the Instagram grid is the benchmark. The page should feel like an extension of the brand (photos, colors, clean typography).
- **Mobile-first** — desktop layout is secondary. The tap target should be obvious and thumb-friendly.
- **Immediate value** — the first thing they see should be services + book now. Not a login wall, not a dashboard. The business profile page IS the product.
- **WhatsApp CTA** — prominent "Chat on WhatsApp" button alongside "Book now." This is how Algeria communicates.

**Recommendation:** The public business page (leenya.dz/salon-name) should be beautiful enough to put in an Instagram bio without embarrassment. This is the primary distribution mechanism — the bio link IS the marketing channel.

## Q6: Landing page & marketing repositioning

### Headline and value prop

Current framing: "Queue management" → targets walk-in businesses.
Recommended framing: **"Your booking page. In your bio."** or **"Accept appointments. Reduce no-shows. One link."**

The pitch to a clinic/salon owner:
> "Your patients already look for you on Instagram. Give them one link to book, see your services, and message you on WhatsApp. No app to download, no phone calls."

### Two distinct landing narratives or one?

**Evidence suggests two landing angles is over-engineering.** The core product (booking page + reminders + scheduling) is the same for clinics and higher-end salons. The queue is a secondary module. One landing page that leads with **"online booking for clinics, dentists, and salons"** with sub-pages for each vertical is cleaner than two entirely different narratives.

**But:** The barbershop/walk-in customer needs a different message. If Leenya keeps serving barbershops, a separate landing path ("manage your walk-in queue online") makes sense. For the main brand repositioning, lead with bookings and mention queue as a feature.

### Recommended positioning language

| Current | New |
|---|---|
| Queue management system | Appointment booking & client management |
| Join the queue, get called, TV display | 24/7 online booking, smart reminders, no-show protection |
| For barbers and salons | For clinics, dentists, beauty professionals |
| Pricing tiers with usage caps | Free to start. Paid at 1,990 DZD/mo. |

### Who to speak to on the landing page

**Primary buyer:** The clinic/salon owner (25–45, has an Instagram account for their business, currently using WhatsApp to manage appointments, frustrated by no-shows, open to "a link for booking" but skeptical of "a software system"). 

**Tone:** Not "enterprise software." More like "a better way to manage your appointments that works with how you already operate." The language should be in French primarily, with Arabic and English alternatives — Algeria's business language is French for commerce, Arabic for public, English growing.

## Summary of recommendations

| Decision | Recommendation | Confidence | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Target segment** | Clinics/dentists first, then higher-end beauty salons | High | Per-visit revenue 2–13× barbers, appointment-first culture, no-show pain is acute |
| **Bookings vs queue** | Bookings is the real product. Queue is a secondary module. | High | Competitors lead with booking; clinics have zero walk-in culture; no-show reduction is #1 pain |
| **Pricing** | Free (1 staff, 100 appts/mo) + Paid at 1,990 DZD/mo (~$15) | Medium | Matches global pricing ($15/mo), trivial for clinics/salons, covers infrastructure costs for moderate usage |
| **Distribution** | Bio link (Instagram/TikTok), WhatsApp-native | High | 14M+ Instagram users in Algeria, social commerce is the fastest-growing channel, bio link is already familiar |
| **Competitive strategy** | Be the layer on top of WhatsApp, not a replacement | High | WhatsApp is the default; Leenya adds structure (booking page, reminders, analytics) that WhatsApp lacks |
| **Landing page** | Lead with booking + bio link. Queue as secondary feature. French primary. | Medium | Two separate narratives overcomplicates; one clear message with vertical sub-pages |

### Open questions / thin evidence
- **dentdz pricing** — not public. Could be free/cheap and undercut Leenya's value prop for clinics. Needs direct competitive analysis.
- **Beautify/Dalti pricing** — 1 month free, then unclear. If they're free forever with premium features, pricing strategy needs adjustment.
- **Willingness to pay for software in Algeria** — the psychological barrier of "first SaaS bill ever" is real even if the math works. This needs customer interviews to validate.
- **Outside Algiers adoption** — bio link distribution assumes Instagram/TikTok presence. In smaller wilayas, Facebook (84.9% share) is dominant. Bio link distribution may need a Facebook-friendly approach too.
